Kitchen Water Damage Help in Sheboygan

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. Our Sheboygan crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

What It Looks Like

  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
  • Opening the sink cabinet releases a stale, damp odor
  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Mold can begin in the dark, closed space under the cabinets and behind the dishwasher.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Stop the Source

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Sink Drain Leaks

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. The water carries food waste, which is why a wet sink cabinet smells and molds so fast.

The Dishwasher Cavity

Slowly dripping supply or drain fittings soak the cabinet sides beside the unit and the subfloor below. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. A leak there wets drywall and framing that the cabinets cover completely, so mold can spread across the wall unseen.

Under the Base Cabinets

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.
