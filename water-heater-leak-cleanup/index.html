Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

In Sheboygan, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. Closet walls and the platform under the tank are usually soaked at the bottom. First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceElectric units get shut down at the breaker. For gas, turn the control knob to off. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWater from the tank can scald. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • An aging steel tank whose bottom is eaten through by corrosion and sediment.
  • Connections that were not fully tightened during a repair or a new installation.
  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • Drain valves that crack, work loose or stop sealing completely.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Garage Wall Base

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Closet Walls

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. Swollen baseboards and a wall bottom that is still damp within are typical.

Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. The water heater itself usually is not, since wearing out with age is considered maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.
