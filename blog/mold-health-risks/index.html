What Mold Does to Your Health

Along with irritants and allergens, mold can in some cases release mycotoxins, which are toxic. These substances can enter your body through inhalation, skin contact, and ingestion. The severity of health effects depends on the type and concentration of mold, duration of exposure, and individual sensitivity. People often report headaches, skin rashes, throat and eye irritation, wheezing, coughing, and nasal congestion. Reactions can be more severe in anyone who has allergies, asthma, or a weakened immune system.

People at Highest Risk

Mold exposure can affect anybody, but the risk is higher for elderly people, infants and young children, anyone with allergies or an immune system disorder, people on immunosuppressive medications, and those with a respiratory condition like COPD or asthma. For these vulnerable populations, prompt mold remediation is especially critical.
